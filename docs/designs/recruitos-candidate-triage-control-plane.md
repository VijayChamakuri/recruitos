**Review history.** Cross-model cold read (Codex gpt-5.6-sol) plus three rounds of independent adversarial spec review: 6/10 → 7/10 → 8/10, with the remaining eight findings applied. Defects the reviews caught and this version fixes: escalation modeled as a terminal state; a scoring function named but never defined; a structural fairness guarantee the architecture did not provide; a flagship demo interaction that would have left the score unchanged; a `derive_level` rule that was non-monotonic in contradicting evidence; perturbation and injection evals that could not run on fixtures; an effort estimate roughly half the real number.

## Problem Statement

A recruiter is managing one urgent Applied AI Engineer req: 120 inbound candidates and 20 sourced candidates in a single week. Every morning they must import new candidates, identify who meets hard requirements, build a shortlist for the hiring manager, explain why each candidate is recommended, flag candidates with missing evidence or low confidence, draft follow-ups, move candidates to the right ATS stage, prepare interview packets for approved candidates, and produce a CEO-ready pipeline summary by Friday.

The work that should disappear is not hiring judgment. It is **manual evidence compilation**: turning scattered resumes, notes, job requirements, scorecards, pipeline status, and recruiter messages into structured, reviewable candidate packets.

| Persona | Task | Frequency | Estimated cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter | Read resume, compare to JD, pull evidence, write summary, decide who needs review, prep next action | Daily during an active req | 5-10 min per candidate, worse at volume |
| Hiring manager | Read packet, understand the recommendation, spot gaps, prepare interview focus | Before loops and debriefs | 15-30 min per candidate when evidence is scattered |
| Talent lead | Find stuck roles, pending reviews, inconsistent screening decisions, misallocated recruiter time | Daily or weekly review | 30-60 min per cycle |
| CEO / founder | Hiring status, top candidates, risks, bottlenecks, decisions waiting on leadership | Weekly or during urgent pushes | High; information spans ATS, notes, interviews, Slack |

RecruitOS automates preparation and coordination. It does not automate final judgment.

**Project intent.** A flagship portfolio project simulating ownership of an internal AI recruiting platform inside a fast-growing company. The evaluation audience is an engineering team deciding whether the author could own their internal AI tooling. This does not lower the bar: the fastest way to fail that audience is to build something a real recruiter would never use.

## Demand Evidence

**Stated honestly: the workflow is inferred, not observed.** The author has not shadowed a recruiter. The workflow is reconstructed from a job description, common ATS and recruiting-operations patterns, and candidate-side experience. This limitation is a product requirement, not a footnote: RecruitOS ships a `WORKFLOW_ASSUMPTIONS.md` stating what is synthetic, what is inferred, and what needs validation.

Independent evidence that the pain is real: a recruiter given a bare percentage with no reasoning **opens the resume and reads it anyway**, so an unexplained score adds a step rather than removing one. A match score without a reason cannot be challenged, defended, or improved, and the recruiter cannot tell whether it came from relevant skills, keyword gaming, or a proxy correlated with a protected attribute. ([match score problem](https://www.heymilo.ai/blog/ai-match-score-problem-explainable-screening), [recruiter trust in algorithmic screening](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9298741/))

**Validation questions for a real recruiter or talent lead:** (1) Which ATS stages do you actually use for this role? (2) What do hiring managers ignore unless summarized? (3) Which screening decisions are safe to automate, and which require approval? (4) What candidate evidence matters most for technical roles? (5) Where do candidates get stuck most often? (6) What follow-ups are repetitive enough to draft automatically? (7) What would make you distrust an AI-generated candidate recommendation?

## Status Quo

**Inside the company:** resumes and notes scattered across the ATS, Notion or Docs, recruiter DMs, and Slack. Evidence compilation is manual copying. Scorecards are filled out shortly before the debrief.

**In the market (searched 2026-09-05):**

- **Ashby**: AI job-criteria matching, AI candidate search with generated filters, natural-language report building, AI notetaker, talent rediscovery (last two are paid add-ons). ([source](https://www.heymilo.ai/blog/automate-ai-candidate-screening-and-engagement-in-ashby))
- **Greenhouse**: Talent Filtering across resumes and notes, AI candidate matching, candidate authenticity checks, AI analytics, conversational voice interviewing post Ezra AI Labs. ([source](https://www.peoplepilot.io/blog/greenhouse-alternatives-2026))
- **Lever**: trails both on AI-native workflows; strength is CRM nurture automation.
- **Metaview** (4,000+ orgs incl. Brex, Deel, Quora, Robinhood): transcribes interviews, maps answers to the role rubric, writes structured scorecards back to 60+ ATSs. A scribe, not an evaluation engine. ([source](https://www.metaview.ai/resources/blog/best-interview-intelligence-software-2026))

**The gap.** All of them produce a score or a note. None expose the decomposition: rubric dimensions, the quoted spans behind each, a confidence value derived from evidence coverage, and a human approval gate with an audit record. RecruitOS is an **intelligence and trust layer above the ATS**, never a replacement.

**Regulatory status quo (materially affects design):**

- **NYC Local Law 144**, in force (effective Jan 2023, enforced Jul 2023, up to $1,500 per violation per day). An AEDT is any tool producing a score, classification, or recommendation. Requires an annual independent bias audit for disparate impact by sex/ethnicity/race, a published summary, and 10 business days of candidate notice. ([DCWP FAQ](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dca/downloads/pdf/about/DCWP-AEDT-FAQ.pdf), [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/services/audit-assurance/articles/nyc-local-law-144-algorithmic-bias.html))
- **Four-fifths rule**: flag when a group's selection rate falls below 80% of the highest group's. Unreliable in small samples. ([analysis](https://www.prevuehr.com/resources/insights/adverse-impact-analysis-four-fifths-rule/))
- **EU AI Act**: recruitment and candidate evaluation are high-risk under Annex III 4(a); Annex III deadline moved from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 December 2027** (Digital Omnibus). Obligations map onto this architecture: human oversight (Art 14), logging (Art 12), transparency to deployers (Art 13). ([source](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/annex-iii/))
- **Structured scorecards** have higher predictive validity and lower demographic disparity than unstructured evaluation; the structure drives most of the gap. ([Metaview](https://www.metaview.ai/resources/blog/create-effective-interview-scorecards), [bias auditing in resume screening](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11548))

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Primary user:** the recruiter running the urgent Applied AI Engineer req. **Secondary:** the hiring manager reading the packet.

**Wedge: the candidate triage packet, end to end.** One role, 140 synthetic candidates, nothing stubbed.

Pipeline order (revised: hard requirements resolve *after* extraction, because work authorization and similar requirements need evidence):

```
parse → dedupe → extract → resolve_hard_requirements → score → confidence → route → shortlist → propose
```

**Increment 2:** Friday leadership summary. **Increment 3:** interview packet and rubric-anchored scorecard generation.

## Constraints

- Synthetic data only. `AtsPort` and `LlmExtractionPort` are implemented; `EmailPort`, `CalendarPort`, `SlackPort`, `SourcingPort` are declared and deliberately unimplemented, documented as such.
- Every AI action grounded in role, candidate, rubric, or audit data. Uncertainty escalates rather than bluffs.
- Human approval required for stage changes, interview decisions, ATS-facing rejections, sensitive decisions, and final recommendations. *Internal triage status is distinct from an outbound rejection: `rejected_hard_requirement` is a triage status visible only inside RecruitOS. Telling a candidate anything requires an approved `Proposal(type: rejection)`.*
- **Never cut:** evidence spans, review proposals, audit logs, evals.
- TypeScript end to end. Python is more idiomatic for eval work, but splitting the runtime costs the single-command demo.

## Premises

**P1. The LLM never emits the score.** The extractor returns, per rubric dimension, an **ordinal level** from a fixed enum anchored to committed rubric text, plus the verbatim quotes justifying it. Never a free-form number, percentage, or aggregate. The aggregate is deterministic code over levels and weights. Reproducible, unit-testable, tunable by weight without touching prompts, defensible in a bias audit.

**P2. The candidate packet is the primary artifact.** The ranked list is a view over packets.

**P3. RecruitOS is a system of engagement over the ATS system of record.** It never owns candidate state. Every outbound effect is a proposal.

**P4. Confidence is computed, never self-reported.** Formula and committed constants below.

**P5 (REVISED). Escalation is a routing outcome that opens a resolution task, not a terminal state.** A candidate leaves a triage run as `scored`, `rejected_hard_requirement`, or `escalated`; escalated is not finished.

| Reason code | Trigger predicate |
|---|---|
| `missing_evidence:<dimension>` | Zero located spans of any polarity for a dimension with `required: true` |
| `missing_evidence:<hard_requirement>` | Hard requirement resolved `unknown` |
| `contradiction:tenure_vs_claim` | Structural: claimed years exceed date-derived years by > 12 months |
| `parse_failure` | A required section is absent, or zero spans located across all documents |
| `ambiguous:seniority` | Two source documents yield seniority levels differing by ≥1 step, or stated title and date-derived tenure disagree by ≥1 step |
| `possible_duplicate` | Near-duplicate score ≥ `T_DUPE` against an already-imported candidate |
| `prompt_injection_flagged` | Source text matches the committed heuristic pattern list (best-effort signal, not a detector) |
| `low_confidence` | `confidence < T_ESCALATE` with no other predicate matched |

`low_confidence` exists so P5's promise that every escalation carries a named reason holds for threshold-only crossings.

**Status precedence.** Multiple predicates can fire on one candidate. `CandidateTriageResult.status` resolves by the committed precedence `rejected_hard_requirement > escalated > scored`, and **every matched predicate opens its own `ResolutionTask` regardless of which one set the status**. Without this rule the Class 1 routing test would be non-deterministic.

Original run stays immutable; the corrected result supersedes it. **An escalation rate near zero is a bug, and so is a rate near 100%.** The rate is not asserted as a free-standing number; it is *derived* from the committed per-candidate expected statuses of the 25 tier-1 candidates and asserted as an exact match against that distribution, with a stated design target of **35-60% escalated on tier 1** as both floor and ceiling. A rate above the ceiling collapses the `scored` population that `shortlist_cut` draws from, which is why only `required` dimensions escalate when unevidenced (see Rubric shape).

**P6 (SHARPENED, two claims of different kinds).**
*Structural, for the scorer:* `f`'s input type is `{dimension_id, level}[]` plus weights. School, name, location, and dates are not representable in that type. (`supporting_span_count` and `contradicting_span_count` are inputs to `derive_level`, not to `f`; keeping them out of `f`'s signature keeps the claim exactly as narrow as it is true.) Asserted by a type-level test and a CI import test, not a runtime perturbation.
*Empirical, for the extractor:* the extractor reads raw text and could leak a proxy into a level. Covered by a **counterfactual perturbation measurement** (below), which is a dated one-shot measurement against the live model, not a CI test.
Every `RubricDimension` carries a required non-empty `job_related_justification`.

**P7. Bias monitoring is built on synthetic data and labeled as such.** Reported at config-defined gates (default: the shortlist cut) with per-cell counts beside every rate, on **both** the proposed cut and the human-approved cut, side by side, because the delta between them is the product's whole thesis. **At n=140 with a shortlist near 10, the primary finding will be "insufficient n for a stable impact ratio," and the report says so in those words.** Demographics are assigned to all 140 candidates so the gate population is complete.

**P8. One command, no API key.** The full loop, including escalation resolution and re-scoring, runs with the deterministic layer and zero credentials. One resolution action (*request re-extraction*) is live-mode only and is labeled in the UI; a single pre-scripted corrected-document variant ships with a recorded fixture so the demo path exists without a key.

**Refinement A.** Score and confidence breakdowns are the default candidate view, not a modal.

**Refinement B.** The packet preserves disagreement: supporting spans, contradicting spans, and explicit gaps are separate types rendered as separate sections.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Codex (gpt-5.6-sol, high reasoning) read a structured summary cold.

**Steelman.** "RecruitOS is an executable reference architecture for trustworthy AI-assisted hiring... The real product is not candidate ranking; it is a control plane demonstrating how consequential LLM automation can remain reproducible, reviewable, and subordinate to an existing system of record."

**Most revealing line.** P8's one-command requirement. "This reveals the actual customer and product: an engineering evaluator assessing system judgment in one sitting."

**Premise challenged (accepted, see P5).** "Insufficient evidence is not an outcome; it is a request for the next action."

**Dominant failure mode.** "The project fails when the synthetic data, recorded outputs, rubric, and evaluator are all co-designed to agree... The antidote is a small adversarial corpus with visibly imperfect outcomes, not 140 generated rows." This is the design's governing risk, and it reappears in the eval layer as parameter tuning (see Calibration Freeze).

**Disagreement.** Codex argued for 20 hand-crafted candidates instead of 140. Half right: 140 shallow rows prove nothing, but 20 loses queue realism. Resolved as a two-tier corpus. Codex's implied statistical argument does not rescue the four-fifths computation either; see P7.

## Approaches Considered

**A: single Next.js application.** Pipeline inside the web app. **Rejected**: hides the architectural claim; ports get no home; the boundary exists only in prose.

**B: core library + CLI, Next.js as review UI.** Selected.

**C: fully event-sourced control plane.** **Rejected**: projections and replay would consume hours belonging to the corpus and evals. Its best idea (append-only audit, immutable versioned results) is borrowed.

**Honest effort comparison.** A is roughly **90%** of B's cost, not 20%. Corpus authoring, evals, and packet UI dominate both and are identical. B's premium is workspace setup plus the CLI surface, 6-9 hours of the total. B still wins, on a small margin honestly stated rather than an unpriced one.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B, with append-only audit events and immutable versioned results borrowed from C.** Current state stays relational. Defending that choice ("I considered event sourcing; an append-only audit log in a relational model was right at this scale") is a stronger signal than building the more elaborate thing.

  ports/                AtsPort, LlmExtractionPort            (implemented)

  adapters/             mock ATS, fixture LLM (default), live LLM (opt-in)

  bias/                 four-fifths — ONLY consumer of CandidateDemographics

evals/                  imports core directly, no HTTP
fixtures/

`core` has zero imports from `web`; `scoring` has zero imports of `CandidateDemographics`. Both asserted by CI lint rules. Those two checks are the architecture claims made verifiable in seconds.

### Parse (non-LLM, fully deterministic)

Heading-heuristic section segmentation producing `{contact, summary, experience[], education[], skills[], authorization_statements[]}`. Each `experience` entry carries `{title, employer, start: YYYY-MM, end: YYYY-MM | present, raw_text}`. `parse_failure` fires when `experience` is empty or when zero spans are later located across all documents.

**Tenure derivation:** union of monthly intervals across all `experience` entries (overlaps counted once, never summed), `present` resolved to the corpus's frozen `AS_OF_DATE` so reruns are stable. Claimed years are read from summary text via a committed pattern list. `contradiction:tenure_vs_claim` fires when claimed exceeds derived by more than 12 months.

**Normalization** (defines both offset basis and what the UI renders): Unicode NFKC, ligatures expanded, non-breaking spaces and bullet glyphs to plain space, runs of whitespace collapsed to one, trailing whitespace per line stripped, line breaks preserved. Stored as `SourceDocument.normalized_text`. The packet renders this text, so quoted evidence highlights exactly what the offsets index.

### Dedupe

Exact: identical `SourceDocument.content_hash`. Near: trigram Jaccard similarity over normalized text ≥ `T_DUPE = 0.82` (single committed mechanism; the previously listed name-plus-employer alternative is dropped). Comparison is against already-imported candidates in **seeded import order**, which is fixed by the corpus seed, so which member of a pair is flagged is deterministic and documented.

### Hard requirements

Resolved after extraction, by a deterministic resolver over parsed fields plus located spans. Each resolves to `pass | fail | unknown`. **Only `fail` sets `rejected_hard_requirement`.** `unknown` escalates with `missing_evidence:<requirement>` and never rejects. This rule is the design's direct answer to "AI screening quietly loses candidates."

### Rubric shape (v1, committed)

Six dimensions. Three carry `required: true` and escalate when unevidenced; three do not, and simply score `none` with an `EvidenceGap`.

| Dimension | `required` | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Applied ML / LLM systems experience | yes | 3 |
| Production software engineering | yes | 3 |
| Evaluation and measurement practice | yes | 2 |
| Data and pipeline work | no | 2 |
| Ambiguity and ownership signals | no | 1 |
| Communication of technical reasoning | no | 1 |

This is the lever that keeps the escalation rate inside its band. With all six required, SC9's ≥70% coverage target would leave nearly every candidate carrying at least one unevidenced required dimension, every candidate would escalate, and `shortlist_cut` would have no `scored` population to draw from. Each dimension's `job_related_justification` is committed alongside it and is required non-empty by schema (P6).

### The scoring function `f`

```
LEVEL_VALUE = { none: 0.0, weak: 0.33, partial: 0.67, strong: 1.0 }

f(assessments, rubric) =
  100 * Σ(LEVEL_VALUE[a.level] * rubric.weight[a.dimension]) / Σ(rubric.weight[*])
```

Input type carries no free text (P6). Weights and level values are committed config, rendered in the UI beside the arithmetic, versioned with the rubric. The denominator is the sum over **all** dimensions, so an unevidenced dimension sitting at `none` contributes 0 and moving it to `partial` visibly moves the aggregate. On the v1 rubric (weights 3/3/2/2/1/1, total 12), moving the evaluation-practice dimension from `none` to `partial` is +11.2 points, which is what makes SC4 non-vacuous.

Candidates with status `rejected_hard_requirement` still receive a full `ScoreResult`. The score is computed and shown; the status is what withholds them from the shortlist. A rejected candidate whose hard requirement is later resolved by a human therefore needs no rescoring pass to become rankable.

**`derive_level` (committed, used in two places):**

```
derive_level(sup, con) =
  sup == 0     -> none
  con >= sup   -> none
  net = sup - con
  net == 1     -> weak
  net == 2     -> partial
  net >= 3     -> strong
```

Monotonic in both arguments by construction: adding a supporting span can only raise the level, adding a contradicting span can only lower it, and there is no fallthrough branch. This matters because the function is surfaced in the packet as a calibration cross-check and pre-fills the human resolution form, so a non-monotonic rule would be visibly wrong in the two places the product most needs to look rigorous.

Used (a) as a cross-check against the extractor's asserted level, surfaced in the packet and measured as a calibration metric, and (b) as the **default proposed level** in the human resolution form.

### Confidence

```
coverage    = dimensions_with_any_located_span / total_dimensions
              (spans of ANY source — extracted or human)
resolution  = ExtractionRun.spans_located / ExtractionRun.spans_returned
              (frozen extractor property; 0 when spans_returned == 0)
contra_rate = clamp01( contradiction_count / total_dimensions )
miss_rate   = required_fields_missing / total_required_fields

confidence  = clamp01( 0.45*coverage + 0.25*resolution
                     - 0.20*contra_rate - 0.10*miss_rate )
```

`contradiction_count` = **distinct dimensions carrying ≥1 contradicting span, plus the count of structural contradictions** (`contradiction:tenure_vs_claim` and its siblings). Not raw span count: Refinement B actively encourages contradicting spans, and counting them raw would punish exactly the behavior the product is built to reward.

`resolution` is a frozen property of the `ExtractionRun` and counts extractor spans only, in both numerator and denominator. Human-supplied spans never enter it, so resolution can never exceed 1. `coverage` counts located spans of any source, which is the term through which human resolution moves confidence. That split is what makes SC4's "changed confidence" mechanically guaranteed for `missing_evidence:<dimension>` escalations rather than incidental.

`total_required_fields` is the committed list `{years_experience, work_authorization, current_title, employer_history}`. `T_ESCALATE = 0.55`. All constants live in one committed config file; the UI renders the formula term by term with each input value.

**Calibration Freeze.** The constants above are frozen against tier 1's *expected* outcomes before the system runs end to end. The first full run's delta between predicted and actual escalation distribution is recorded in the Trust Center as a calibration result. Tuning the constants until the assertion passes is the "co-designed to agree" failure mode reappearing in the eval layer, and the freeze plus the recorded delta is the defense.

### Quote location (three tiers)

The extractor is prompted for **verbatim contiguous quotes, maximum 240 characters**, and returns text only. Offsets are never accepted from the model; code locates the quote in `normalized_text`:

1. `exact` — byte-identical substring of `normalized_text`
2. `normalized` — match after case folding and quote/dash folding. **All tier-2 transforms are character-for-character offset-preserving** (folding maps one char to one char, never inserting or deleting), so the match index is directly an index into `normalized_text`. Any transform that would change length is disallowed at this tier.
3. `fuzzy` — sliding token-window search over `normalized_text` with stride 1 token, window length spanning ±20% of the quote's token count, scored by **Levenshtein ratio normalized by the sum of both string lengths** (difflib / rapidfuzz `ratio` semantics, not max-length normalization), cutoff ≥ 0.90. Ties among equal-ratio windows break by **lowest start offset**. Offsets are the window's own bounds in `normalized_text`.
4. unlocated — span dropped, quote and reason recorded on `ExtractionRun.dropped_quotes`, confidence falls via the `resolution` term

Every tier returns offsets that index `normalized_text` directly, which is what the Class 1 offset-resolution invariant asserts and what the packet renders.

The `fuzzy` tier exists because LLM quotes routinely paraphrase, truncate, or merge lines, and without it the unlocated rate cascades into a collapsed `resolution` term and an escalation rate far outside the expected distribution. The unlocated rate is itself a reported Trust Center metric.

### Domain model

`Role`; `Requirement(kind: hard | scored)`; `Rubric` / `RubricDimension(id, weight, required: boolean, definition, job_related_justification` non-empty`)`; `Candidate(channel: inbound | sourced, corpus_tag: main | variant, current_result_id)`; `SourceDocument(content_hash, raw_text, normalized_text)`; `EvidenceSpan(document_id, start, end` non-null`, quoted_text, dimension, polarity: supporting | contradicting, source: extracted | human, match_quality: exact | normalized | fuzzy, extractor_version)`; `EvidenceGap(dimension_id, reason_code, documents_searched[]` non-empty`)`; `DimensionAssessment(dimension, level, span_refs[], source: extracted | human, actor_id?)`; `ExtractionRun(spans_returned, spans_located, dropped_quotes[], model_id, fixture_key)`; `ScoreResult`; `TriageRun`; `CandidateTriageResult(triage_run_id, candidate_id, status, score_result_id, supersedes, resolution_task_ids[])`; `ResolutionTask(id, candidate_triage_result_id, reason_code, opened_at, status: open | resolved | dismissed, resolution_type, resolved_by_actor_id, resolved_at, resulting_result_id)`; `Proposal(type: follow_up_draft | ats_stage_change | shortlist_inclusion | rejection, payload, evidence_refs[], confidence, status)`; `ReviewDecision(approve | edit | reject | request_evidence, actor, timestamp, diff)`; append-only `AuditEvent`; `CandidateDemographics` (separate table, imported only by `core/bias`).

`supersedes` lives on `CandidateTriageResult`: re-triage is scoped to the resolved candidate and never creates a new 140-candidate run. `Candidate.current_result_id` is the materialization pointer, moved transactionally with each new result and accompanied by its own audit event. `match_quality: unresolved` does not exist; unlocated quotes are recorded on `ExtractionRun`, which supplies the `spans_returned` denominator.

### Escalation resolution (deterministic, no API key)

| Action | Effect | LLM? |
|---|---|---|
| **Supply evidence and set level** | Inserts an `EvidenceSpan(source: human)`, located by the same code path as extracted spans, **and** writes a `DimensionAssessment(source: human)` for that dimension. The form pre-fills the level from `derive_level` over the new span counts; the human confirms or overrides. | No |
| Correct a parse | Re-runs parse and quote location | No |
| Confirm a judgment | Writes `DimensionAssessment(source: human)` | No |
| Block | Sets status directly | No |
| Dismiss | Closes the task, records rationale | No |
| Request re-extraction | Calls the extractor | **Live mode, plus one pre-scripted fixture-mode candidate** so the path is demonstrable without a key |

Supplying evidence and setting the level is **one atomic operation**, because `f` reads levels, not spans: adding a span alone would move confidence and leave the score unchanged, which would make the flagship demo appear to do nothing. Bundling them is also the honest product design, since assigning a level from new evidence is exactly the judgment a human should be making.

The accurate claim this supports is not "the score is a function of spans." It is: **humans and models write to the same typed structure, through the same validation, into the same audit log, and the score is a pure function of that structure regardless of who wrote it.**

Re-scoring calls `f` and the confidence formula again with no model invocation. The shortlist, the ranking, and the bias report recompute on demand from `Candidate.current_result_id`, are versioned by the latest result per candidate, and a `TriageRun` reports `complete_with_open_tasks` while any `ResolutionTask` remains open.

### Shortlist

`shortlist_cut(results, config)` takes candidates with status `scored`, sorts by `ScoreResult.aggregate` descending, and takes `SHORTLIST_N = 10`. Ties break by candidate id for determinism. **Escalated candidates are excluded from the cut but surfaced in a separate "pending resolution, may qualify" list** so they are visibly not lost. Each shortlisted candidate produces `Proposal(type: shortlist_inclusion)`; the human-approved set is derived from `ReviewDecision`s on those proposals. The bias report measures **both** the proposed cut and the approved cut.

### Follow-up drafts (deterministic, no LLM)

`Proposal(type: follow_up_draft)` payloads are produced from committed templates filled with packet fields (candidate name, role, the specific `EvidenceGap` reason, the requested item). No drafting model call, no `LlmDraftingPort`, and P8 stays intact. Template selection is a pure function of the escalation reason code. LLM-authored drafts are an increment-2 upgrade behind a new port.

### Chat: six saved queries, zero model calls

Six typed functions over SQLite, each rendering a citation block: why this candidate ranks first, which candidates were escalated and why, which meet all hard requirements, what evidence supports this score, which drafts await approval, what changed since the last run. **Selected from buttons, not free text**, so there is no intent-classification model call and no P8 conflict. A free-text box maps via a committed keyword matcher and returns "I can answer these six questions" on no match, never an error. Optional LLM intent classification is a live-mode-only enhancement.

### Persistence and UI

SQLite in WAL mode, Drizzle migrations, single writer at a time (adequate at this scale; CLI and web both write). Six surfaces: triage queue, candidate packet, escalation queue and resolution form, proposal review queue, audit log viewer, Trust Center report. Actor identity is a seeded actor picker writing `actor_id` and `actor_display_name` to every audit event; real authentication is explicitly out of scope.

### Two-tier corpus (25 + 115 = 140)

**Tier 1: 25 hand-authored adversarial candidates** (20 inbound, 5 sourced) with committed per-candidate expected status, expected levels, expected spans, expected reason codes, and expected hard-requirement resolutions. Coverage: contradictory dates, ambiguous seniority, missing years-of-experience evidence, missing work-authorization evidence, exact and near-duplicates, malformed sections, prompt-injection text, definitive hard-requirement failures, strong candidate with weak formatting, weak candidate with keyword stuffing, mixed-evidence cases.

**Tier 2: 115 generated candidates** (100 inbound, 15 sourced). Sourced candidates carry profile-shaped documents, exercising a different parse path.

**Variant corpora** (first-class members with their own recorded fixtures, additional to the 140): 20 perturbation variants (name, school, and location permuted over 20 tier-2 candidates) and 5 injection/control pairs built on tier-1 candidates.

**Variants are namespaced and isolated.** They carry `corpus_tag: variant`, execute in their own `TriageRun`, and are excluded from dedupe comparison, from `shortlist_cut`, and from the Class 3 bias gate population. Without this they would break three things at once: a perturbation variant differs from its control only in name, school, and location, so trigram Jaccard puts it far above `T_DUPE = 0.82` and all 20 would self-flag `possible_duplicate`; they would enter the shortlist ranking; and they would inflate the bias population past the 140 that P7's small-n statement is sized against.

Their recorded fixtures exist so the Class 2 *reported* results are reproducible offline under P8. They are still Class 2 measurements, not CI tests: a frozen fixture makes a perturbation comparison un-failable, which is exactly the category confusion the three-class split exists to prevent.

Demographics are assigned to **all 140** so the bias gate population is complete.

Fixtures cover every corpus document, keyed
`sha256(model_id + prompt_template_version + rubric_dimension_definitions_version + document_content_hash + dimension_id)`.
A miss in fixture mode is a hard error naming the key, never a silent live call. Rubric **weights** are not in the prompt, so weight tuning never invalidates fixtures; rubric **dimension definitions** are, so editing them requires `recruitos fixtures:record` and a re-authoring pass over tier-1 expected levels. That cost is why the recruiter call is sequenced before fixture recording (see Dependencies).

### Trust Center

**Class 1: deterministic invariant tests (CI, on fixtures).** Span offsets non-null and resolvable; `f` correctness against worked examples; rerun determinism (labeled an invariant check, not a quality claim, since fixture replay is deterministic by construction); hard-requirement `pass|fail|unknown` correctness; escalation routing matched against the committed tier-1 expected distribution; resolution loop and supersession; audit completeness and immutability; `core`-has-no-`web` imports; `scoring`-has-no-`CandidateDemographics` import; `f`-input-type-excludes-free-text (type-level).

**Class 2: dated one-shot measurements against the live model** (model ID and date committed, never re-run in CI, and explicitly labeled un-failable in CI): span precision against tier-1 expected spans; unlocated-quote rate; `derive_level` versus extractor level calibration; structural and semantic contradiction recall; **counterfactual perturbation** (perturbed variants versus controls, comparing a canonical `ScoreResult` serialization that excludes ids and timestamps); **prompt-injection resistance** (injected versus control pairs, same comparison).

**Class 3: bias report.** Four-fifths ratio at config gates on both the proposed and approved shortlist cuts, per-cell counts printed, primary finding stated as insufficient n where true.

**When a recorded fixture disagrees with a committed tier-1 expected level**, the resolution is explicit and depends on which artifact is authoritative. `fixtures/expected/` holds two separately-labeled kinds of entry: `assertion` (the level the system *should* produce; disagreement is a Class 1 failure) and `known_limitation` (the level the system *currently* produces, with a written explanation of why it is wrong; disagreement is also a Class 1 failure, because it means behavior drifted). Neither is Class 2. Class 2 measures the live model against the same expected spans and levels and reports a number without passing or failing.

**The Trust Center must not be all green.** At least 3 of the 25 tier-1 candidates are committed as `known_limitation`, so the visible failures are permanent fixtures of the report rather than something to be fixed away before publishing.

## Open Questions

1. **Rubric authoring.** Hand-written v1 committed (leaning), with JD-driven generation as an increment-2 proposal type.
2. **Level enum granularity.** Four levels is a guess; five with a neutral midpoint may calibrate better or may invite default-to-middle bias. Testing it means re-recording all fixtures and re-authoring tier-1 expected levels, so it is scoped to a 25-candidate subset if attempted at all.
3. **`T_DUPE = 0.82` and `SHORTLIST_N = 10`** are starting values requiring empirical selection against the tier-1 near-duplicate pairs and the observed score distribution.

## Success Criteria

A reviewer can, in one sitting, without credentials:

1. Run one command that seeds the corpus, executes triage through `@recruitos/core`, and runs the eval suite
2. Open the review UI and see the triage queue for the role
3. Open any packet and see, as the default view: dimensions with levels, spans quoted against normalized source with resolvable offsets, supporting / contradicting / gaps as separate sections, the `f` arithmetic, and the confidence formula term by term
4. See escalations with named reason codes, resolve one via **supply evidence and set level**, trigger a re-score with no API key, and observe **both a changed aggregate score and a changed confidence** on a superseding `CandidateTriageResult` alongside the preserved original. The demo path is pinned to a named tier-1 candidate carrying a `missing_evidence:<dimension>` reason code, because that is the case where the confidence change is mechanically guaranteed via the `coverage` term. A `low_confidence`-only escalation on a candidate already at full coverage moves the score but not necessarily confidence, and the UI says so rather than implying otherwise.
5. Approve, edit, or reject a proposed follow-up draft
6. Use any of the six saved chat queries and get an answer citing rows, dimensions, spans, or audit records
7. Inspect an immutable audit trail covering every AI action and human decision
8. Read a Trust Center report with real numbers including failures, its three classes clearly separated, and a `WORKFLOW_ASSUMPTIONS.md`
9. **Recruiter-facing check, falsifiable:** across tier 1, **≥70% of rubric dimensions are backed by a located span**, every remaining dimension carries an `EvidenceGap` with a non-empty `documents_searched` and a reason code from a closed enum, and **zero dimensions are silently absent**. Asserted in the eval suite. This is the criterion that decides whether the packet removes a step or adds one.

## Distribution Plan

`git clone`, then `make demo`: seed, triage, eval, UI start. No API key.

CI on push and PR: typecheck, lint, boundary tests, unit tests, Class 1 evals, one Playwright smoke test asserting the packet renders resolvable spans. The eval summary is committed and surfaced in the README, with an explicit note that CI verifies the deterministic layer and fixture replay, not live extraction quality.

| # | Cut | Degrades |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tier 2 from 115 → 50 | Queue realism; worsens the already-conceded small-n bias finding |

## Dependencies

- Live LLM mode requires an Anthropic API key; strictly optional at demo time but **required once** to record fixtures.
### Build sequence (binding)

1. **Get recruiter or hiring-process input** if at all possible (see The Assignment).
2. **Lock the rubric.** Dimensions, `required` flags, weights, and `job_related_justification` text all frozen.
3. **Then record tier-1 fixtures** and author the tier-1 expected outcome sets.
4. **Then build** scoring, confidence, escalation, audit, and UI against the locked rubric.

**Do not record fixtures before the rubric is stable.** Rubric dimension definitions live in the extraction prompt and are part of the fixture cache key, so a later rubric change invalidates all 140 recorded fixtures *and* every tier-1 expected outcome, which means re-paying the 20-28h corpus line item in full. Rubric *weights* are deliberately kept out of the prompt so weight tuning stays free; it is the definitions that are expensive to change.

Schema, pipeline scaffolding, and the deterministic scoring and confidence functions can all be built against a draft rubric v1 in parallel with step 1, because none of them depend on the dimension text. Only fixture recording and expected-outcome authoring must wait.

## The Assignment

**Get one real recruiter or talent lead on a 30-minute call before you record a single fixture.**

The rubric is the one artifact where being wrong invalidates everything downstream: extraction targets, weights, escalation thresholds, every expected outcome, and every fixture keyed on the dimension definitions. Commit a draft v1 so schema and pipeline work is unblocked, but treat v2 as the real one and do not pay for fixtures until it exists.

Recruiters are the most responsive cohort on LinkedIn because responding to strangers is literally their job. Ask a specific favor: "I'm building an open-source tool that turns resumes into evidence-backed candidate packets. Can I show you a rubric for an Applied AI Engineer role and have you tell me what's wrong with it for 20 minutes?" Bring the draft to react to, because reacting is far easier than generating. Ask the seven validation questions, and lead with number seven: *what would make you distrust an AI-generated candidate recommendation?* Their answer is your Trust Center backlog, sourced from a real user instead of from you.

Then update `WORKFLOW_ASSUMPTIONS.md` to move at least three items from "inferred" to "validated" or "corrected," with attribution, and bump the rubric to v2. A repo whose assumptions doc shows corrections from a real conversation is a fundamentally different artifact from one that shows only careful guesses.
