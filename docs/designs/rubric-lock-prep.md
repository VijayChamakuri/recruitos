## What this is

The rubric is the one artifact where being wrong invalidates everything downstream:
extraction targets, weights, escalation thresholds, every tier-1 expected outcome, and
every fixture keyed on the dimension definitions. The binding build sequence puts a
practitioner conversation before the lock for exactly that reason
(`docs/designs/recruitos-candidate-triage-control-plane.md`, The Assignment;
`docs/plans/recruitos-candidate-triage-implementation-plan.md`, Gate 1 and Gate 2).

This document does three things:

1. **Part 1** is the call plan and question set, tailored to the Applied AI Engineer role,
   leading with what would make you distrust an AI-generated candidate recommendation.
2. **Part 2** is a candidate rubric v2 proposal: real definition prose, real
   `job_related_justification` prose, and per-level anchors for all six committed
   dimensions, written to replace the draft v1 placeholders currently in
   `packages/core/src/rubric/draft-v1.ts`.
3. **Part 3** is a readiness note: what is frozen, what is pending, and the exact open
   questions that need a human answer before the lock.

## What this is not

- **Not a lock.** Locking is a human decision that happens after the call, per Gate 2.
- **Not code.** Part 2 is prose staged for a drop-in edit, so that the eventual lock is a
  small and obvious change to one file plus one schema field. Nothing under `packages/`
  is touched by this document.
- **Not validated.** Nothing here is attributed to a practitioner, because no practitioner
  has seen it. The baseline it will be measured against is `WORKFLOW_ASSUMPTIONS.md`.

**Structure is frozen and this document does not change it.** Dimension ids, weights
(3 / 3 / 2 / 2 / 1 / 1, total 12), the required flags (three required), the four-level
ordinal enum, `LEVEL_VALUE`, `f`, `derive_level`, and the confidence formula are all as
committed in the approved design. Only definition prose, justification prose, and level
anchors are drafted here.

# Part 1: The practitioner call

## Who to ask, and for what

Target: one recruiter or talent lead who has personally screened for a technical AI or ML
role. A second call with a hiring manager who has run an Applied AI Engineer loop is worth
more than a longer call with the recruiter, because the two answer different questions and
disagree in useful places.

The ask, kept small and specific because reacting is far easier than generating:

> I'm building an open-source tool that turns resumes into evidence-backed candidate
> packets instead of a match percentage. I have a draft screening rubric for an Applied AI
> Engineer role. Can I show it to you for 20 minutes and have you tell me what's wrong
> with it? No prep needed, and nothing you say is used for a product pitch.

**Ground rules for the call, from the implementation plan's Gate 1:** a realistic synthetic
role only. No real candidate data is discussed, shown, or accepted. If the practitioner
starts describing a specific real candidate, redirect to the pattern.

**Attribution.** Ask at the end, not the start: name and role, role and company stage only,
or fully anonymous. Default to anonymous if they hesitate.

## Timeboxes for 30 minutes

| Minutes | Segment | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Framing, ground rules, permission to record notes | Sets the synthetic-only boundary before anything else |
| 2-8 | **Q0, the distrust question** | Leads, because their answer becomes the Trust Center backlog |
| 8-16 | Q1 through Q6, workflow reality | Q4 must land **before** the rubric is shown, so their answer is unanchored |
| 16-26 | Rubric react pass, R1 through R5 | The draft is on screen; they react rather than generate |
| 26-29 | Calibration exercise, C1 | The highest-value five minutes on the call |
| 29-30 | Close, attribution, follow-up ask | |

If the call runs short, cut in this order: R5, then Q5, then Q3. Never cut Q0, Q4, R3, or
C1.

## Q0. The lead question

> **A tool hands you a candidate packet for your Applied AI Engineer req and says this
> person is a strong match. What would make you not believe it? And what is the first
> thing you would go check?**

Follow-ups, asked only as needed:

- Has an automated screen ever been wrong about a candidate you liked? What happened, and
  what did you do differently afterward?
- What would a tool have to show you before you would forward a candidate to the hiring
  manager without opening the resume yourself? Is there any such thing?
- Is there anything a tool could put in front of you that makes your job *worse* than
  having no tool at all?
- If the tool were wrong about someone, how would you find out? Would you ever find out?

**Why it leads.** Their answer is the Trust Center backlog sourced from a real user instead
of from the author. It also directly tests WA-19 through WA-22, which are the product's
central bets: that verbatim spans, separated contradictions, visible arithmetic, and a
computed confidence buy trust rather than noise.

**Listen for:** whether their distrust is about *accuracy* (the tool got the facts wrong)
or about *authority* (the tool made a call that was not its to make). Those two answers
point at different products. The design assumes the second.

## Q1 through Q6: the six validation questions, sharpened for this role

The approved design lists seven validation questions. Q0 above is the seventh. These are
the other six, rewritten so they cannot be answered in the abstract.

**Q1. Which ATS stages do you actually use for this role?**

> Walk me through the stages an Applied AI Engineer candidate moves through in your ATS,
> from application to offer. Which of those do you change by hand, and which move
> automatically? Where does an inbound applicant sit during the first 48 hours, and who
> is responsible for them there?
>
> Follow-up: do you keep any working state about a candidate that isn't an ATS stage, in
> a spreadsheet or your head or a Slack thread? If a tool kept that state for you and
> never touched the ATS without your approval, is that useful or is it a second system to
> maintain?

Tests: WA-01, WA-05, WA-06. The follow-up is the real question, because premise P3 stakes
the whole architecture on the answer being "useful".

**Q2. What do hiring managers ignore unless you summarize it?**

> For an Applied AI Engineer req specifically, what do you end up writing in the note to
> the hiring manager that they would never get from the resume itself? And what do they
> come back and ask you every single time that you should have put in there?
>
> Follow-up: when they push back on a candidate you forwarded, what is their reason
> usually? Is it something you could have seen on the resume?

Tests: WA-04. Their "asks me every time" list is a candidate rubric dimension the draft may
be missing.

**Q3. Which screening decisions are safe to automate, and which need approval?**

> For this role, draw me the line. Which of these would you let a tool do without you
> looking first: send a follow-up asking a candidate for a missing detail, move someone
> into a review queue, mark someone as not meeting a hard requirement, reject someone,
> tell someone they were rejected. Where is the line, and what would move it?
>
> Follow-up: is the line different for an inbound applicant than for someone you sourced?

Tests: WA-05, WA-18. Also validates the human-approval constraint list in the design's
Constraints section.

**Q4. What candidate evidence matters most for technical roles?**

> **Ask this before showing the rubric.** For an Applied AI Engineer specifically, what do
> you actually read for on a resume, in what order? What is the fastest signal that
> someone is worth a call? What is the fastest signal that they are not?
>
> Follow-up: which of those signals do you trust, and which do you use because it's fast
> even though you know it's noisy?

Tests: WA-08, WA-09, WA-15. This is the single most important question for the rubric, and
its value depends entirely on asking it before the draft is on screen. An anchored answer
is worth almost nothing here.

**Q5. Where do candidates get stuck most often?**

> Think about the last month on a technical req. Where did candidates sit longest without
> anyone acting on them? What caused it, and who unblocked it?
>
> Follow-up: how many candidates in a week do you end up unsure about, where you would
> want more information before deciding? What do you do with them today?

Tests: WA-23, WA-26. The follow-up is a direct read on tolerable escalation volume, asked
without naming the number first.

**Q6. What follow-ups are repetitive enough to draft automatically?**

> What message do you write over and over for a role like this? Would you be willing to
> send me two of them with the names taken out, so the templates in this thing sound like
> a person wrote them?
>
> Follow-up: when you ask a candidate for something missing, what are you usually asking
> for?

Tests: the follow-up-draft template set. The missing-item list should map onto the
`EvidenceGap` reason codes, and any gap they name that has no reason code is a finding.

## R1 through R5: the rubric react pass

Show the draft. Screen share or a one-page printout. Reveal it in this order, because the
order controls what they anchor on.

**R1. The six dimensions, names and definitions only. No weights, no required flags.**

> Here are six things this tool tries to find evidence for. What is missing? What does not
> belong? What would you call these instead, in your words?

**R2. Now the weights.**

> Rank these six by what actually predicts a good hire for this role. Just order them, do
> not worry about numbers.
>
> Then: here is what I guessed. Where am I wrong?

Ranking before revealing is deliberate. Weights are free to change (they never enter the
extraction prompt, so tuning them never invalidates a fixture), so this is the cheapest
correction available and the one most worth collecting.

**R3. The required and not-required split, explained honestly.**

> Three of these are marked so that if the tool finds no evidence at all, it stops and
> puts the candidate in front of you instead of scoring them low and moving on. The other
> three just score zero and note the gap.
>
> I have to be straight about how I picked: I picked partly on what seemed right and
> partly because if all six stopped and asked you, every candidate would stop and ask you
> and the tool would be useless. So: if this thing finds no evidence at all for a
> dimension, which ones do you want it to interrupt you for?

Tests: WA-11 directly. Their answer may conflict with the escalation-band constraint. See
Part 3, Known tensions.

**R4. Level anchors for one dimension, read aloud.**

Use `applied_ml_llm_systems`. Read the `partial` and `strong` anchors.

> Is `strong` something you could tell from a resume? Or is that a thing you only know
> after a phone screen? If it is not reachable from a resume, tell me, because a level
> nobody can ever reach is worse than not having it.

Then the same question for `ambiguity_and_ownership` and `communication_of_reasoning`,
which is where the honest answer is most likely to be "no".

Tests: WA-13. If both behavioral dimensions fail this question, the honest fix is to drop
them from v2 rather than keep them at weight 1 for completeness.

**R5. Hard requirements.**

> The tool treats work authorization, years of experience, and location as pass, fail, or
> unknown, and it never rejects on unknown. Are those the right three for this role? And
> the important one: does work authorization ever actually appear on a resume, or is that
> only ever an application form question?

Tests: WA-16, WA-17. If work authorization is never in the document, the requirement
resolves `unknown` for nearly every candidate and floods the escalation queue with a
question the corpus cannot answer. That is a concrete, checkable, corpus-invalidating
finding, and it is worth the two minutes even if R5 is otherwise the first thing cut.

## C1. The calibration exercise

Five minutes, and the highest-signal part of the call.

Bring two short synthetic resume excerpts, roughly 150 words each, printed. One should be
strong-but-badly-formatted; one should be keyword-stuffed-but-thin. Both come from the
tier-1 corpus plan.

> Read this. For "applied ML and LLM systems", would you call the evidence here none,
> weak, partial, or strong? Now for "evaluation and measurement".

Record their level verbatim, and the phrase they point at when they justify it. Then say
what the draft anchors would produce and ask where the difference is.

**Why this matters more than anything else on the call.** It is the only part that produces
a *comparable* datum rather than an opinion. Two humans assigning levels to identical text
is the closest thing available to inter-rater reliability, and the whole rubric rests on
the claim that the levels mean something outside the author's head. It also directly tests
WA-12, four levels versus five: if the practitioner hesitates between two adjacent levels
and wants something in between, that is evidence for a five-level enum, and if they pick
confidently, that is evidence to close the design's open question at four.

## Close

- If we get this rubric wrong, what breaks for you first?
- Anything I did not ask that I should have?
- Would you look at the actual packet screen for ten minutes in a couple of weeks? (This
  is the `/design-review` and `/qa` follow-up, and it is worth asking for now while they
  are warm.)
- Attribution preference.

# Part 2: Candidate rubric v2 proposal

**Proposal only.** This replaces the `definition` and `jobRelatedJustification` placeholder
strings in `packages/core/src/rubric/draft-v1.ts` and adds level anchors, after the call
and after the human decides. Dimension ids, weights, and required flags below are copied
verbatim from the committed structure and are not up for change in this document.

## Cross-cutting reading rules

These apply to every dimension and are part of what the extractor is told.

1. **Levels describe located evidence, not the candidate.** A `none` level means the
   documents provided did not evidence this dimension. It is not a claim about the person.
   The packet copy must say it that way.
2. **Self-description is never evidence.** "Strong communicator", "thrives in ambiguity",
   "expert in LLMs" are claims about the candidate made by the candidate, with no action,
   artifact, scope, or outcome attached. They do not raise a level above `none` on their
   own. This rule is deliberate and it is the rubric's main defense against the keyword
   gaming the design's tier-1 corpus specifically tests.
3. **A skills-list keyword is not evidence.** A tool name in a list, with no system it was
   used on, is `none`.
4. **Anchors and `derive_level` are different instruments and are supposed to disagree
   sometimes.** The anchors describe evidence *quality* and are what the extractor reads.
   `derive_level(sup, con)` counts located spans and has, in the design's own words, no
   scoring authority; it is rendered in the packet as a labeled calibration diagnostic.
   Where they disagree, the packet shows both. Anchors written to make `derive_level`
   always agree would destroy the diagnostic's only use.
5. **Contradicting evidence is a separate polarity, not a lower level.** A contradicting
   span is recorded as contradicting and rendered in its own section. It is an input to
   `derive_level` and to the confidence formula's contradiction term. It never silently
   nets out.
6. **Quotes are verbatim, contiguous, and at most 240 characters.** Offsets come from code,
   never from the model.

## 1. `applied_ml_llm_systems` (weight 3, required: true)

**definition**

> Evidence that the candidate has built, shipped, or operated systems in which a machine
> learning model or a large language model is a load-bearing component of product
> behavior. Covers model integration and serving, prompt and context design, retrieval,
> tool use and agent loops, fine-tuning or adaptation, inference latency and cost work,
> and handling the failure modes specific to non-deterministic components. Evidence is a
> named system, the candidate's own described contribution to its model-bearing part, and
> something concrete about how it behaved. A tool name, a course, or a claim of
> familiarity is not evidence.

**job_related_justification**

> The role's daily work is building internal tooling in which model output drives product
> behavior that people act on. A candidate who has only called a model API inside a
> notebook has not met the failure modes this role hits in its first week:
> non-determinism, context limits, cost under load, and output that is confidently wrong.
> Weighted 3, jointly highest, because it is the half of the role title that cannot be
> substituted by adjacent experience.

**Level anchors**

- **none**: no located span of any polarity ties the candidate to a system in which a
  model was a working component. Skills-list mentions, coursework, and certifications with
  no system attached leave this at `none`. Because this dimension is `required`, a `none`
  level with zero located spans opens `missing_evidence:applied_ml_llm_systems` and routes
  the candidate to a human rather than silently scoring zero.
- **weak**: machine learning or LLM work is named but stays unanchored. A project title
  with no system behind it, a hackathon or coursework build, a tools inventory, or a claim
  of involvement with no described responsibility, scope, or result.
- **partial**: at least one named system with a real user or a real business purpose, in
  which the candidate describes their own contribution to the model-bearing part, but the
  text leaves scope, scale, production status, or outcome unstated.
- **strong**: a system in production with users, in which the candidate owned model-bearing
  behavior, and the text names concrete detail: the approach or architecture, plus at
  least one of scale, latency, cost, output quality, or failure handling, with an outcome
  attributable to the candidate rather than to the team in general.
- **contradicting evidence looks like**: date-derived tenure that cannot support the
  claimed years of ML work; a stated title with no engineering ownership sitting beside a
  claim of owning the system; the same system described with incompatible scope in two
  documents.

**Ask the practitioner**: is `strong` reachable from a resume for this dimension, or is it
a phone-screen judgment? (R4)

## 2. `production_software_engineering` (weight 3, required: true)

**definition**

> Evidence of writing, reviewing, shipping, and operating software that other people
> depend on. Covers language and systems fluency demonstrated on a named system, testing,
> code review, continuous integration, deployment, monitoring, on-call and incident
> response, migrations, and continued ownership of a codebase after launch, including code
> the candidate did not write. Evidence is a system with users and a described engineering
> practice attached to it, not a list of languages.

**job_related_justification**

> The systems this role builds are production software with real users, an audit trail,
> and consequences when they are wrong. The role is expected to own a service end to end
> rather than hand a prototype to a platform team. Weighted 3, equal to applied ML,
> because an applied AI engineer who cannot operate what they build produces demonstrations
> rather than tools, and this project's own thesis is that the difference between those
> two is the whole product.

**Level anchors**

- **none**: no located span of any polarity ties the candidate to software that other
  people used. Because this dimension is `required`, `none` with zero located spans opens
  `missing_evidence:production_software_engineering` and routes to a human.
- **weak**: engineering appears only as language and framework names, or the only shipped
  work is a personal or course project with no described users, no collaborators, and no
  responsibility that continued past the build.
- **partial**: the candidate shipped software that other people used and describes their
  own engineering contribution to it, but the text says nothing about how it was tested,
  reviewed, deployed, or operated after release.
- **strong**: the candidate shipped and then operated software that others depended on, and
  the text names concrete practice (tests, review, continuous integration, deployment,
  monitoring, on-call, a migration, performance or reliability work) attached to a named
  system, with responsibility that continued past launch.
- **contradicting evidence looks like**: a stated title or described scope that is
  non-engineering across the same period; employment dates that cannot support the claimed
  years of engineering; a claim of production ownership against a described contract scope
  limited to prototyping.

**Ask the practitioner**: does this read as a separate thing from dimension 1, or as the
same thing said twice? Two dimensions at weight 3 that a screener conflates would put 6 of
12 total weight on one signal.

## 3. `evaluation_and_measurement` (weight 2, required: true)

**definition**

> Evidence that the candidate measures whether a system works rather than asserting that
> it does. Covers offline and online evaluation, test sets and golden data, metric
> definition, labeling and annotation processes, holdout or A/B design, regression
> detection, error analysis, calibration, and drift monitoring, with particular weight on
> measuring components whose output is not deterministic. Evidence names what was
> measured, against what, and what decision followed.

**job_related_justification**

> This is the dimension that separates an applied AI engineer from a model API consumer,
> and it is the same practice this role must apply to its own output: the role ships
> automation that makes consequential recommendations about people, and without
> measurement it ships a system whose failures nobody can name. Weighted 2 rather than 3
> because a strong production engineer can be brought into a measurement practice faster
> than the reverse. Marked `required` despite the lower weight because absence of any
> measurement signal is the most common and most consequential gap in this candidate pool,
> and it deserves a human look rather than a silent zero.

**Level anchors**

- **none**: no located span of any polarity relates to measuring a system's behavior.
  Because this dimension is `required`, `none` with zero located spans opens
  `missing_evidence:evaluation_and_measurement` and routes to a human. This is the design's
  named demo path, because it is the case where supplying evidence and setting a level
  moves both the aggregate score and the confidence.
- **weak**: measurement appears only as a noun or a claim. "Metrics", "A/B testing",
  "data-driven decisions", a dashboard, or a benchmark number the candidate did not
  produce, with no named metric, no dataset, and no decision that turned on it.
- **partial**: the candidate names a specific metric, test set, or evaluation they ran for
  a system they worked on, but the text does not say how the comparison or ground truth was
  constructed, or what changed as a result.
- **strong**: the candidate built or owned an evaluation for a system whose correctness was
  not self-evident, and the text names what was measured, how the ground truth or baseline
  was obtained, and a decision or change that followed from the result.
- **contradicting evidence looks like**: measurement claimed for a project the same
  document elsewhere describes as never launched; an evaluation described in terms
  incompatible with the stated system; a metric claimed as owned that another document
  attributes to a different team.

**Ask the practitioner**: R3, specifically. Do you want to be interrupted when a candidate
shows no evaluation signal at all, or is that just a low score?

## 4. `data_and_pipeline_work` (weight 2, required: false)

**definition**

> Evidence of building or operating the data path a product or model depends on. Covers
> ingestion, parsing and extraction from messy or unstructured sources, schema and storage
> design, transformation, batch and streaming pipelines, data quality checks, and backfills
> or migrations. Evidence is a data path the candidate changed or owned, with something
> said about what it moved and who consumed it. Use of a warehouse somebody else built is
> not evidence of building one.

**job_related_justification**

> This product is a parsing and normalization problem before it is a model problem:
> candidate documents arrive in inconsistent formats and the pipeline is what makes them
> scorable at all, which is why quote location has three tiers and why normalization is a
> committed policy. Weighted 2 because that work is a large and unglamorous share of the
> role. Not `required` because a strong applied ML engineer may have worked entirely on
> top of a platform team's data path, which is a real gap in the score but not a reason to
> interrupt a human.

**Level anchors**

- **none**: no located span of any polarity relates to moving, shaping, or storing data.
  Because this dimension is not `required`, `none` records an `EvidenceGap` with its
  `documents_searched` list and contributes 0 to the aggregate, without opening a
  resolution task.
- **weak**: data work appears only as tool names (SQL, Spark, Airflow, dbt, a named
  warehouse) or as consumption of a pipeline someone else built, with no described
  contribution to how the data arrived.
- **partial**: the candidate built or changed a specific data path and describes what it
  produced, but volume, reliability practice, ownership duration, or downstream consumer
  is unstated.
- **strong**: the candidate owned a data path in production that other systems or people
  depended on, and the text names the sources, the transformation or schema decisions, and
  at least one of volume, reliability practice, data quality handling, or a migration or
  backfill they ran.
- **contradicting evidence looks like**: pipeline ownership claimed against a described
  analyst or reporting scope; volume or scale figures that disagree between two documents
  about the same system.

**Ask the practitioner**: R3. Would you want an interrupt on a missing data signal? If the
answer is yes, see Part 3, Known tensions.

## 5. `ambiguity_and_ownership` (weight 1, required: false)

**definition**

> Evidence that the candidate has taken a problem that arrived underspecified and driven it
> to a decided outcome: choosing the scope, making a call without a full specification,
> crossing a team boundary to unblock it, and staying with the work past the interesting
> part. Read from what the candidate did and what followed, never from how the candidate
> describes themselves. "Self-starter", "thrives in ambiguity", and an unqualified "led"
> are self-description and are not evidence.

**job_related_justification**

> The role is an early internal build where nobody has written the specification and the
> requirements arrive as a recruiter's frustration rather than as a ticket. Weighted 1,
> the lowest available weight, precisely because resume text is thin evidence for it: every
> point of weight placed here would be a point taken from a dimension the documents can
> actually support. It is kept in the rubric because it is genuinely job related and its
> absence should be visible in the packet; it is kept small and not `required` because
> escalating on it would route a human to a question a resume cannot answer.

**Level anchors**

- **none**: no located span describes work the candidate initiated, scoped, or decided.
  Self-description with no action attached leaves this at `none`. Not `required`, so this
  records an `EvidenceGap` and contributes 0.
- **weak**: ownership appears only as self-description, or as an unqualified verb ("led",
  "drove", "owned") with no problem, no decision, and no outcome attached to it.
- **partial**: the candidate names specific work they initiated or scoped, and an outcome,
  but the text does not show what was undecided at the start or what call they made about
  it.
- **strong**: the text names a problem that arrived without a specification, a decision the
  candidate made about scope or approach, and what followed from that decision, including
  work carried past the point where it stopped being interesting.
- **contradicting evidence looks like**: a claim of sole ownership beside a description of
  the same work as a team deliverable; described decision authority that the stated role
  and tenure cannot support.

**Ask the practitioner**: R4. Is any of this readable from a resume, or is this an
interview dimension wearing a resume dimension's clothes? A clear no is a reason to cut it
from v2, not a reason to keep it at weight 1.

## 6. `communication_of_reasoning` (weight 1, required: false)

**definition**

> Evidence that the candidate can make technical reasoning legible to someone who will not
> read the code. Covers design documents, requests for comment, incident write-ups,
> documentation named as a deliverable, talks, published technical writing, teaching and
> mentoring with a described audience, and, as directly observable evidence, the
> candidate's own account of a technical decision and the reason for it in their
> application materials. A stated communication skill is not evidence.

**job_related_justification**

> The output of this role is an explanation a recruiter has to be able to trust and a
> hiring manager has to be able to challenge, so the person building it has to be able to
> write one. Weighted 1 because the resume-stage signal is thin and easily confounded by
> professional resume writing or editing help, which is a confound this rubric cannot
> detect and should not pretend to. Not `required`, for the same reason. The interview loop
> is where this dimension is actually assessed; the rubric's job here is only to notice
> when the evidence is unusually strong.

**Level anchors**

- **none**: no located span speaks to producing technical explanation for other people.
  Not `required`, so this records an `EvidenceGap` and contributes 0.
- **weak**: communication appears only as a self-assessment, as an implied duty of a job
  title, or as an artifact named without an audience or a purpose.
- **partial**: the candidate names a specific artifact they wrote or delivered for an
  identified audience (a design document, a postmortem, documentation, a talk, teaching),
  but the text does not carry any of the reasoning that artifact contained.
- **strong**: the text carries reasoning the candidate authored, in which a technical
  decision is stated together with the reason for it and what it was chosen over, whether
  that appears inside a named artifact or in the candidate's own account of their work.
- **contradicting evidence looks like**: an authorship claim on a document another source
  attributes to someone else; a described audience that the stated role could not have
  reached.

## Known tensions to resolve on the call

**T1. The `required` split is engineering-driven and the call may contradict it.**
The design is explicit: the split is the lever that keeps the escalation rate inside its
35 to 60 percent band, because with all six `required` every candidate would escalate and
`shortlist_cut` would have no `scored` population. If the practitioner says a missing
data-pipeline signal warrants an interrupt, or that a missing evaluation signal does not,
there is a genuine conflict between what a user wants and what the demo needs. Resolve it
in favor of the practitioner and re-derive the escalation band, or resolve it in favor of
the band and record the disagreement as a known limitation in the Trust Center. Do not
resolve it silently in favor of the band, which is the failure the whole project is built
to argue against.

**T2. Two dimensions may not survive contact.** `ambiguity_and_ownership` and
`communication_of_reasoning` rest on WA-13, the weakest assumption in the file. They carry
2 of 12 total weight between them. If the practitioner says neither is readable from a
resume, cutting them is the honest move, and it changes the frozen structure. That makes it
a design-level change requiring a decision record, not a prose edit. It is listed here so
the possibility is priced before the call rather than discovered during it.

**T3. Adding level anchors expands what the rubric commits to.** Anchors are prose about
what evidence looks like. If they enter the extraction prompt, they are part of the fixture
cache key and become as expensive to change as definitions. If they stay in documentation,
the extractor is calibrated by definition text alone and the anchors serve only the human
reviewer and the packet UI. Both are defensible; the choice must be explicit before Gate 4
because it changes what a fixture re-record costs.

## Open questions requiring a human answer before lock

### Requiring practitioner input (Gate 1)

- **OQ-1. Are these six the right dimensions, and what is missing?** Q4 and R1 test it.
  Blocks WA-09. The specific worry is a missing dimension around domain or product
  judgment, or around working with non-engineers, both plausible for an internal tooling
  role and both absent from the draft.
- **OQ-2. Are the weights right, and specifically should `evaluation_and_measurement` be 3
  rather than 2?** R2 tests it. Blocks WA-10. Cheap to change even after lock, because
  weights never enter the prompt.
- **OQ-3. Is the required and not-required split right?** R3 tests it. Blocks WA-11. See
  tension T1.
- **OQ-4. Are `ambiguity_and_ownership` and `communication_of_reasoning` readable from a
  resume at all?** R4 tests it. Blocks WA-13. See tension T2.
- **OQ-5. Is `strong` reachable from a resume for each of the six, or are some `strong`
  anchors describing an interview-stage judgment?** R4 tests it. A level nobody can reach
  is worse than a missing level, because it silently compresses the top of the scale.
- **OQ-6. Would a practitioner assign the same levels to the same text?** C1 tests it.
  Blocks WA-12 and, more importantly, blocks the claim that the levels mean anything
  outside the author's head.
- **OQ-7. Are work authorization, years of experience, and location the right hard
  requirements, and does work authorization ever appear in a resume?** R5 tests it. Blocks
  WA-16 and WA-17. A "no" on work authorization is corpus-invalidating and needs to be
  known before tier-1 authoring, not after.
- **OQ-8. What escalation volume is tolerable?** Q5's follow-up tests it. Blocks WA-26. The
  current band implies roughly 49 to 84 resolution tasks per 140-candidate week.
- **OQ-9. What would make you distrust the recommendation?** Q0. Does not block the rubric
  lock, but it is the input to the Trust Center backlog and it is the reason the call is
  worth having at all.
- **OQ-10. Is "escalated" the right word?** Blocks nothing structural, cheap now, expensive
  after UI copy lands.

### Requiring a product-owner decision (no practitioner needed, still human)

- **OQ-11. Do level anchors enter the extraction prompt?** See tension T3. Recommendation:
  yes, with anchors carried in a versioned field, because the anchors are the calibration
  and an extractor asked for an ordinal level without them is guessing. The cost is that
  anchors join definitions as expensive-to-change, which is acceptable if they are locked
  in the same pass.
- **OQ-12. How are anchors represented in the schema?** The current `RubricDimensionSchema`
  has `dimensionId`, `weight`, `required`, `definition`, `jobRelatedJustification`, and
  nothing else, and is `.strict()`. Carrying anchors needs one added field. Recommendation:
  a `levelAnchors` object keyed by the four level values, each non-empty prose under the
  existing 2000-character bound, so the four-level enum stays the single source of truth
  for which keys exist. The alternative (a documentation-only sibling constant) keeps the
  schema untouched but lets the anchors drift away from the rubric they describe.
  **`packages/**` is owned by a parallel workstream right now; this is a named change, not
  an applied one.**
- **OQ-13. What are `rubricId` and `version` for v2, and how does the Trust Center label
  provenance?** The plan requires the Trust Center to label the rubric as product-authored
  rather than practitioner-validated when the `RubricAssumptionRecord` fallback is used.
  That means provenance is a rendered property of the locked rubric and needs somewhere to
  live.
- **OQ-14. Four levels or five?** The design lists it as open question 2 and notes a
  five-level scale with a neutral midpoint may calibrate better or may invite
  default-to-middle bias. Recommendation: close it at four, and let C1 supply the evidence.
  Testing five means re-recording every fixture and re-authoring every tier-1 expected
  level, which the design already prices as scoped to a 25-candidate subset if attempted at
  all.

## The lock, when it happens

Kept deliberately small, which is the point of staging it this way.

1. Run the call. Fill the note-taking template verbatim.
2. Update `WORKFLOW_ASSUMPTIONS.md`: move at least three entries out of `inferred`, keep

8. Only then Gate 3 (tier-1 corpus and expectations) and Gate 4 (fixture recording). Not
